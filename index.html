BPRONUNCIATION AND GRAMMAR

1. Pronunciation: /br/, /kr/ and /tr/

Key consonant clusters
• /br/: breadwinner, breakfast, brown
• /kr/: crash, crane, cream
• /tr/: track, tree, train
• Pronounce both consonants clearly; do not add an extra vowel between them.

2. Present simple vs. present continuous

NỘI DUNGPRESENT SIMPLEPRESENT CONTINUOUS

DẤU HIỆUusually, often, always, sometimes, every day/week, once/twice a weeknow, right now, at the moment, today, this week; Look! Listen!
LƯU ÝStative verbs thường không dùng ở tiếp diễn: know, understand, like, want, need, agree, believe, remember...always + present continuous có thể diễn tả lời phàn nàn: He is always leaving his socks on the floor.

3. Expressing opinions

Useful language
• Strong opinion: I strongly believe that ... / I have no doubt that ...
• Neutral opinion: In my opinion, ... / To my mind, ... / I suppose that ...
• Disagreement or uncertainty: I’m not sure about that.

C. Pronunciation – Sentence stress

In normal speech, content words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives and adverbs) are usually stressed. Function words are normally unstressed unless the speaker wants to contrast or correct information.

C. Pronunciation – Rhythm

English rhythm is created by alternating stressed and unstressed syllables. Important content words usually receive stress, while grammar words are often reduced. Speakers may change the normal rhythm to correct or contrast information.

  • DON’T PICK the FLOWERS when you GO to the PARK.
  • The STUDENTS CLEAN the SCHOOL PLAYGROUND every WEEKEND.
  • WHAT is HAPPENING with the POLAR BEARS?
  • I SAID AIR pollution, not WATER pollution.

D. Grammar – Reported speech

TYPEUSEFORMEXAMPLE
Reported statementsreport what somebody saidsaid (that) + clause; told + object + (that) + clauseNam said that he was preparing a presentation.

Common changes when the reporting verb is in the past: present simple → past simple; present continuous → past continuous; past simple / present perfect → past perfect; will → would; can → could.

C. Pronunciation – Intonation

Statements and Wh-questions usually have a falling tone. Yes/No questions often have a rising tone. Speakers can change the usual tone to show surprise, uncertainty, politeness, or strong interest.

  • We are going on an eco-friendly FIELDTRIP. ↘
  • What should TOURISTS do to protect the CAVE? ↘
  • Can we bring reusable BOTTLES? ↗
  • You would touch the CORAL? ↗ (surprise)

D. Grammar – Conditional sentences Type 1 and Type 2

TYPEUSEFORMEXAMPLE
Conditional Type 1real or possible present/future situationsIf + present simple, will/can/may + base verbIf visitors follow the trail, they will protect the plants.
Type 1 negativea possible result when a condition is not metIf + present simple, will not/cannot + base verbIf tourists leave litter, the beach will not stay clean.
Conditional Type 2imaginary or unlikely present/future situationsIf + past simple, would/could/might + base verbIf I lived near the park, I would work as a local guide.
Advice with Type 2give advice politelyIf I were you, I would / would not + base verbIf I were you, I would bring a reusable bottle.
Questions with Type 1ask about a possible resultWhat will + subject + do if + present simple?What will you do if the tour is cancelled?
Questions with Type 2ask about an imaginary situationWhat would + subject + do if + past simple?What would you do if you saw someone touching coral?

Use Conditional Type 1 for real or possible situations in the present or future. Use Conditional Type 2 for imaginary, unlikely, or impossible situations in the present or future.

In Type 2, were can be used with all subjects, especially in formal English: If I were you, ...; If the village were closer, ...
